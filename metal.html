B.Tech Metallurgical & Materials Engineering (MME) Study Guide

Metallurgical and Materials Engineering (MME) is the science and technology of processing, designing, and manufacturing metals, alloys, ceramics, polymers, and advanced composites. From aerospace superalloys and automotive steels to semiconductor silicon and biomaterials, materials engineering underpins all modern engineering innovations.

Core Metallurgical Syllabus Domains

  • Physical Metallurgy & Phase Transformations: Understand crystal structures (BCC, FCC, HCP), Miller indices, and point/line defects. Master unary and binary phase diagrams, Gibbs phase rule, Lever rule, the Iron-Iron Carbide (Fe-Fe3C) equilibrium diagram, Time-Temperature-Transformation (TTT) diagrams, Continuous Cooling Transformation (CCT) curves, and heat treatments (annealing, normalizing, hardening, tempering, austempering).
  • Thermodynamics & Kinetics of Materials: Laws of thermodynamics applied to metallurgical systems, Ellingham diagrams for oxide and sulfide stability, free energy-composition curves, activity and chemical potential, Raoultâ€™s and Henryâ€™s laws, and diffusion mechanisms governed by Fickâ€™s First and Second Laws.
  • Mechanical Behavior & Deformation: Elasticity, plasticity, dislocation theory, slip systems, Schmid's law, twinning, strengthening mechanisms (solid solution, grain boundary Hall-Petch strengthening, precipitation hardening, work hardening), creep mechanisms, fatigue failure (S-N curves, Paris law), and Griffith's theory of brittle fracture.
  • Extractive & Process Metallurgy: Principles of pyrometallurgy, hydrometallurgy, and electrometallurgy. Blast furnace ironmaking thermodynamics, basic oxygen furnace (BOF/LD) steelmaking, secondary refining, and non-ferrous extraction processes (Bayer process and Hall-HÃ©roult electrolytic cell for aluminum, matte smelting for copper).
