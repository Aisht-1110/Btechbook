Comprehensive Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulic Machinery Guide

Fluid Mechanics is a foundational pillar of mechanical, civil, chemical, and aerospace engineering. This curriculum guide covers core theoretical principles, governing equations, unit-by-unit syllabus breakdowns, key formulas, and high-frequency university exam questions aligned with AICTE, Anna University, VTU, and AKTU syllabi.

Unit-by-Unit Theoretical Revision

Unit 1: Fluid Properties & Fluid Statics

A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously under the application of a shear (tangential) stress, no matter how small. Unlike solids which resist shear with finite deformation, fluids in motion exhibit continuous strain rates.

  • Mass Density (ρ): Mass per unit volume (kg/m³). For water at 4°C, ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
  • Specific Weight (γ): Weight per unit volume, γ = ρ · g (N/m³). For standard water, γ ≈ 9810 N/m³.
  • Specific Gravity (S): Dimensionless ratio of fluid density to standard fluid density (water for liquids, air for gases). S = ρ_liquid / 1000.
  • Dynamic Viscosity (μ) & Newton's Law: Shear stress is directly proportional to velocity gradient: τ = μ (du/dy). SI unit is Pa·s or N·s/m² (1 Poise = 0.1 Pa·s).
  • Kinematic Viscosity (ν): Ratio of dynamic viscosity to density: ν = μ / ρ (m²/s). (1 Stoke = 10⁻⁴ m²/s).
  • Surface Tension (σ) & Capillarity: Tensile force acting on fluid surface per unit length (N/m). Capillary rise/depression height is given by h = (4σ cos θ) / (ρ g d).
  • Pascal’s Law: Intensity of pressure at any point in a static, incompressible fluid is equal in all directions.
  • Hydrostatic Law: Rate of pressure increase in the vertical downward direction equals specific weight: ∂P/∂z = ρ g.
  • Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle: Any body submerged wholly or partially experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced (F_B = ρ g V_disp). Stable equilibrium of floating bodies requires the Metacenter (M) to lie above the Center of Gravity (G), i.e., GM > 0.

Unit 2: Fluid Kinematics & Flow Descriptions

Fluid kinematics analyzes fluid motion without considering the forces causing the motion. Flow fields are mapped using Eulerian and Lagrangian frames of reference.

  • Flow Regimes: Steady flow (∂V/∂t = 0) vs Unsteady flow; Uniform flow (∂V/∂s = 0) vs Non-uniform flow; Laminar flow (viscous, low Re, smooth layers) vs Turbulent flow (chaotic eddies, mixing).
  • Flow Lines: Streamlines (tangent to velocity vector at every point), Pathlines (trajectory traced by an individual fluid particle), and Streaklines (instantaneous locus of particles passing through a fixed point). In steady flow, all three coincide.
  • Continuity Equation (Conservation of Mass): In 3D Cartesian coordinates for compressible unsteady flow:
    ∂ρ/∂t + ∂(ρu)/∂x + ∂(ρv)/∂y + ∂(ρw)/∂z = 0.
    For steady incompressible flow: ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y + ∂w/∂z = 0, or in 1D pipe flow: A₁ V₁ = A₂ V₂.
  • Velocity Potential (Φ) & Stream Function (Ψ): Velocity potential exists only for irrotational flow (curl V = 0). Stream function exists for any 2D incompressible flow. Orthogonal intersection between equipotential lines and streamlines forms a Flow Net.

Unit 3: Fluid Dynamics & Governing Conservation Laws

Fluid dynamics investigates the relationship between fluid motion and the forces acting upon it (gravity, pressure, viscosity, and turbulence).

  • Euler's Equation of Motion: Derived from Newton's second law along a streamline neglecting viscosity:
    (dP / ρ) + v dv + g dz = 0.
  • Bernoulli’s Equation: Integrating Euler’s equation for steady, incompressible, frictionless (inviscid), and irrotational flow gives:
    (P / ρg) + (v² / 2g) + z = Constant
    Where (P/ρg) is pressure head, (v²/2g) is velocity/kinetic head, and z is datum/potential head.
  • Flow Measurement Devices:
    • Venturimeter: Employs convergent-divergent duct to measure flow rate: Q_act = C_d [a₁ a₂ / √(a₁² - a₂²)] √(2gh), where C_d ≈ 0.96 - 0.98.
    • Orifice Meter: Compact plate with concentric orifice with vena contracta, C_d ≈ 0.60 - 0.65.
    • Pitot Tube: Converts kinetic head to stagnation pressure head: v = C_v √(2gΔh).
  • Momentum Equation: Based on impulse-momentum theorem, total force on a fluid control volume equals net rate of momentum outflow: ΣF = ṁ(v_out - v_in). Critical for calculating jet impact forces on turbine blades and pipe bends.

Unit 4: Flow Through Pipes & Dimensional Analysis

  • Reynolds Number (Re): Ratio of inertia forces to viscous forces: Re = (ρ V D) / μ = (V D) / ν. For internal pipe flow: Re < 2000 (Laminar), 2000 ≤ Re ≤ 4000 (Transition), Re > 4000 (Turbulent).
  • Hagen-Poiseuille Law (Laminar Pipe Flow): Pressure drop ΔP = (32 μ V_avg L) / D². Maximum centerline velocity is exactly twice the average velocity: u_max = 2 V_avg.
  • Darcy-Weisbach Equation (Major Friction Loss): Head loss due to friction: h_f = (f L V²) / (2 g D), where Darcy friction factor f = 64 / Re for laminar flow.
  • Minor Losses: Caused by geometric disruptions: Sudden enlargement (h_L = (V₁ - V₂)² / 2g), Sudden contraction (h_L = 0.5 V₂² / 2g), Bends, elbows, and valves (h_L = k V² / 2g).
  • Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL) vs Total Energy Line (TEL): HGL plots (P/ρg + z) (piezometric head); TEL plots (P/ρg + v²/2g + z) (total energy head). The vertical distance between TEL and HGL is always the velocity head v²/2g.
  • Buckingham's π Theorem: Dimensional analysis technique where n physical variables involving m fundamental dimensions can be grouped into (n - m) independent dimensionless π-terms.

Unit 5: Boundary Layer Theory & Hydraulic Turbines

  • Prandtl’s Boundary Layer Concept: In high Re flows past solid walls, viscous effects are confined to a thin layer adjacent to the surface. Velocity rises from zero at the wall (no-slip condition) to 99% of freestream velocity U_∞ at thickness δ.
  • Displacement Thickness (δ*): Distance wall must be displaced outward to compensate for fluid mass deficit: δ* = ∫₀^δ (1 - u/U_∞) dy.
  • Momentum Thickness (θ): Loss of momentum flux caused by friction: θ = ∫₀^δ (u/U_∞)(1 - u/U_∞) dy.
  • Hydraulic Turbines Classification:
    • Impulse Turbine (Pelton Wheel): High head (> 150m), low flow rate. Entire pressure head converted to kinetic jet at atmospheric pressure.
    • Reaction Turbines (Francis & Kaplan): Medium to low head (< 150m), high discharge. Fluid enters under pressure in spiral casing and pressure drops across runner. Francis is mixed radial-inflow; Kaplan is pure axial flow with adjustable blades.
  • Centrifugal Pumps: Dynamic pumps that convert mechanical shaft power into kinetic energy via impeller rotation, then into pressure head in volute casing or diffuser vanes. Cavitation occurs when suction pressure drops below liquid vapor pressure P_v.

High-Frequency University Exam Questions

Q1: State the assumptions in deriving Bernoulli's equation from Euler's equation.

The four primary assumptions are: (1) The fluid is ideal and non-viscous (μ = 0); (2) The flow is steady (∂/∂t = 0); (3) The fluid is incompressible (ρ = constant); and (4) The flow is irrotational (curl V = 0), meaning the Bernoulli constant holds across all streamlines.

Q2: What is the physical significance of Metacentric Height (GM)?

The metacentric height (GM) measures the initial static stability of a floating vessel. When a body tilts by an angle θ, buoyant force acts through the center of buoyancy creating a righting couple if M is above G (GM > 0, stable), zero couple if M coincides with G (GM = 0, neutral), and an overturning moment if M is below G (GM < 0, unstable).

Q3: Explain the difference between Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL) and Total Energy Line (TEL).

The Total Energy Line (TEL) represents the sum of pressure head, velocity head, and datum elevation along the pipe. The Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL) represents only piezometric head (P/ρg + z). The TEL always lies above the HGL by an amount equal to velocity head V²/2g. Due to pipe friction losses, both lines slope downwards in the direction of flow.

Q4: What causes cavitation in hydraulic turbines and centrifugal pumps, and how can it be prevented?

Cavitation occurs when local static pressure falls below the vapor pressure of the liquid at that operating temperature. Liquid boils, creating vapor cavities that collapse violently when moving into higher pressure zones, eroding runner blades and causing vibration. It is prevented by maintaining positive Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH) above NPSH required, using cavitation-resistant stainless steel alloys, and avoiding excessive suction lift heights.
